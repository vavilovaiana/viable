The brief

Crypto and everyday money — in one app

Quppy is a European fintech app: people open an IBAN account, fund it by card or bank transfer, hold and exchange crypto, and send money via SEPA, Faster Payments or to a card. The app was already live, but it grew faster than its interface — new features were stacked on top of old logic, and people got lost.

  • Audit the live app and propose changes for every journey.
  • Build a complete app map — from registration to account deletion.
  • Redesign onboarding, wallets, exchange, sending and receiving money.
  • Hand developers a UI kit with documented component states.

The Challenge

What we had to solve
  • Fiat accounts and crypto wallets follow different rules, yet must feel like one product.
  • Network and exchange fees were hidden — people found out after the transaction.
  • Losing the mnemonic means losing the money, and backup was offered too late.
  • New features — mobile top-up, wallet reissue, KYC — had to fit in without overload.

Key findings

No clear first step Right after opening the app people need “Create a wallet” and “I already have a wallet” in front of them.
Hidden fees Sent 2 EUR but more was charged — the network fee only appeared after the transfer.
Amounts hard to scan Both the coin and the USD amount are needed, aligned right — otherwise small ETH fees disappear.
Import by phrase only A wallet could only be imported with the recovery phrase, not with a private key.
No feedback After an operation people didn't know what had happened or what to do next.
Unclear terms “Address type”, “parent mnemonic”, “another operator” — questions instead of actions.

Onboarding

Six screens, six reasons to stay

Instead of an abstract “welcome to a new financial reality”, each screen shows one concrete feature through a 3D illustration built from the app's own UI. “Create an account” and “I have an account” are available on every step.

Key journeys

Money and crypto, one set of rules

Journey 01

Dashboard & wallet

Total balance in BTC and USD on top; below it, crypto wallets and IBAN accounts as separate groups with an All / Wallets / Accounts filter. Each wallet has three main actions and a visible but calm backup reminder.

  • Coin amount and its equivalent — always right-aligned
  • Mobile top-up surfaced as a banner on the dashboard
Journey 02

Exchange & send crypto

Exchange reads as “from → to”, with the minimum and maximum right under the input. Sending no longer starts with copying an address: recent recipients and your own wallets are shown up front, and an address can be pasted or scanned as a QR code.

  • Network fee visible before confirming
  • New addresses can be saved to the address book
Journey 03

Fiat transfers

Sending starts with choosing a route — to an IBAN or to a card — and tells you right away when the recipient gets the money. Receiving became a ready-to-share set of bank details: IBAN and receiver copy in one tap, bank details sit in an expandable block, and Share is the main action.

  • Branches for “recipient exists / doesn't” and “card exists / doesn't”
  • Processing time on every transfer screen
Journey 04

Card funding & mobile top-up

When funding from a card, people see limits, the fee and the total before tapping “Receive funds”. In mobile top-up the main risk is a contract number the money will never reach, so the warning sits right above the contact list.

Journey 05

Security & verification

Before the mnemonic is shown, people tick three rules: find a safe place, don't take a screenshot, write it down. The KYC form asks only for what's needed to open a fiat account, and registration checks each password rule live while typing.

New feature · 2024

Wallet reissue: the fee before, not after

A wallet can be reissued for a 1 USDT fee, and its address changes. We put both facts before the confirmation: people choose which wallet pays the fee and only then confirm. The flow covers “reissue one” and “reissue all”, with success and error states.

Visual communication

Store listing, illustrations and website

App Store and Google Play screenshots tell the story in five promises and were produced in 8 languages. For illustrations we put together a moodboard of clean, vivid yet professional 3D art and tested it on website hero concepts.

Outcome

What the Quppy team got

One logic for money

Crypto and fiat share the same patterns: from → to, amounts on the right, the fee before confirming.

A map for developers

19 sections with branches and arrows — the team sees how people reach every screen.

A UI kit

Components with states instead of one-off mockups — new features are assembled from ready blocks.

Room to grow

The card, wallet reissue and new funding methods fit in without rebuilding the navigation.
